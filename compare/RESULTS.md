# Same traffic, two admission models

Generated by `python3 -I compare.py --report`. Cells: mean (min–max) over seeds 1, 2, 3. Traffic: Onur's workload (honest 0.05/s, attacker 0.2/s, uniform honest topics, all.json IPs, 24 h). Pocc 10 unless stated.

How to read every table:

- Only new arrivals are shared: the same seed gives every model the same arrival times, IPs, topics and attacker IDs. Renewals (one per expired ad) and retries depend on each model's admissions, so total traffic differs by model.
- "honest admitted / started" (Onur's success rate) counts renewals in the denominator, so its population differs by model. "new honest requests admitted" uses new arrivals only, which are the same in every model.
- Delay percentiles and starvation (> 2E) count admitted requests only. Requests still waiting at the 24 h horizon appear in neither. go quotes up to 24 h while Onur caps a quote at E, so go can leave more slow requests out of these rows.
- go: attackers wait out every quote. go+give-up: every request, attacker or honest, follows the client's 1.5·E budget. go+honest-give-up: only honest requests follow it.

## Notes

Models: onur-paper is Onur's `Registrar` unmodified. onur-current replaces only `required_wait` with w = base·c(s)/(c+1) + base·score + base·G, base = 0.1·E/(1−c/C)^Pocc, and keeps the rest of Onur's mechanics: credit counted from the first attempt, no lower bounds, quotes capped at E, admission only when w ≤ 1e-9 s, a full cache quoting E. go is the sim-admission port of topdisc 90b0983 brought to 703d5a2 in a subclass: G = 1e-7 and `scaleBase` (#170), and the ticket key lifecycle of ticket.go (a key rotates after 100,000 tickets and is dropped 6 h after its last issuance; a ticket whose key is gone starts a new attempt without credit, as in v5_udp.go). All models use Onur's event loop and workload, and every quoted attempt retries at now + max(quote, 1 s). In go, the retry presents the returned ticket. Every go ticket presented with a live key landed inside its window; none was early, late or expired. The port already expires ads at the request's time (#173) and drops a topic's bound with its last ad (#177, which reverted #166); an earlier run of this grid kept #166 and is in grid-pre177.csv and RESULTS-pre177.md (see "Change from #177" below).

Reproduction: seed 1 of onur-paper matches REPRO.md exactly: co300 C=1000 38.2 % / 21,122 s; C=5000 52.6 % / 8,100 s; tt C=1000 35.0 % / 32,413 s; tt C=5000 52.2 % / 13,427 s; co10k IP on / off 37.8 % / 83.0 %; /16 36.8 %; /8 36.4 %. `test_compare.py` also checks that compare.py and Onur's script give the same summary row.

onur-paper → onur-current: the new function divides the base by 10 (0.1·E instead of E), so more requests get in. At C=1000 under cache occupation, utilisation goes from 28.5 % to 40.9 %, the attacker share from 37.6 % to 43.8 %, and the honest p95 from 21,141 s to 18,080 s. With IP score off the two give nearly the same attacker share (82.6 % vs 83.1 %).

onur-current → go, same function:
- At C=5000 the attacker share agrees within 1 pp at Pocc 10 (60.4 % vs 60.1 % co300; 60.9 % vs 60.8 % tt). Across the Pocc sweep the share agrees within 1.9 pp, utilisation within 2.9 pp, and honest p95 within 9.2 %.
- Attempts per admission: go needs about 3, onur-current about 10 at C=1000. Onur caps a quote at E and computes it again on each retry. Go quotes the whole remaining wait (up to 24 h) on a ticket, so one quote and one retry usually suffice.
- At C=1000, go runs a lower utilisation (34.1 % vs 40.9 %) and attacker share (39.9 % vs 43.8 %). A go request keeps its quote for the whole wait, and the topic and IP lower bounds stop later quotes from dropping when occupancy falls. In onur-current a quote lasts at most E.
- IP score off, 10,000 attacker topics: here the models split. In go, 43.5 % of new honest requests are admitted within 24 h at C=1000 and 19.9 % at C=5000; in onur-current 75 % and 88 %. At C=5000 the cache stays fuller than with IP score on (73.5 % average utilisation), so base is far larger: near 82 % occupancy it is about 2.5e9 s, where one ad of the topic (base/(c+1), about 6e5 s) reaches the 24 h cap while an empty topic pays only base·G, about 250 s. The 10,000 attacker topics are almost always empty (9,634 of 313,664 fresh attacker quotes had an ad of the topic in the cache). In seed 1, all 2,101 honest quotes at the 24 h cap were for a topic with an ad in the cache, and 2,702 of 2,869 fresh honest admissions were for a topic with none, mostly renewals whose own ad had just expired. go's 913 s honest p95 at C=5000 covers only the requests that were admitted (see "How to read every table"). Onur's model has no bounds or tickets and its E cap re-quotes the honest request every E, so it gets in once occupancy drops.
- Ticket keys: 40 of the 162 go runs present tickets whose key is gone, up to 2,110 per run (co300-p16 C=1000 with honest give-up). Keys rotate after 100,000 tickets; a ticket quoted for more than 6 h outlives its key once the registrar has rotated. In the pre-#177 run that Codex audited (co10k-noip, C=1000, seed 1) there were 16 such presentations, and honest admissions dropped from 1,517 to 1,516.
- Duplicates: go answers a request for an (advertiser, topic) already in the cache with `existing` (WaitTime E, no ticket), and the Go client treats that as registered (v5_topic.go, HandleRegistered). The request ends there as admitted, with no second cache entry; the ad already in the cache keeps its own renewal. Attacker IDs come from a pool of 100,000, so on the single target topic go sees on average 101 such replies per run at C=1000 and 504 at C=5000. In the other experiments it is at most 7 per run. Onur's models admit the duplicate as a second ad. (Before review round 1 the request came back after E as a fresh attempt, which gave 146 and 2,046 replies per run because a returning duplicate could meet its own ad again; see "Review round 1" below.)
- Admission threshold: Onur admits when w ≤ 1e-9 s; Go admits when the remaining wait is ≤ 1 s. This is only an eligibility threshold. An earlier admission changes occupancy and every later quote, so admission times can differ by much more than 1 s (Codex found 214 s for one request).

Give-up (the client's 1.5·E budget: a single quote or the running total above 1,350 s drops the attempt). An abandoned request comes back after E as a fresh attempt with no ticket, as in sim-admission's run.py. Its delay still counts from the first attempt. "Abandon events" counts each give-up, so one request can add several. The three go columns assume different attackers:
- go: nobody gives up. Attackers wait out every quote, which in these cases is their better option against a single registrar (compare go+give-up).
- go+give-up: attackers obey the client budget too. With IP score on and /24, /16 or /8 attackers, the attacker share falls to 6.3–6.7 % (C=1000) and 24–26 % (C=5000). 95 % of attacker requests at C=1000 give up at least once: base·score on a crowded prefix is above 1,350 s, and after each restart the request again gets a quote over budget. This holds only if attackers follow the honest client code.
- go+honest-give-up: only honest requests give up; attackers wait out every quote. The attacker share rises above go (49.0 % vs 39.9 % at C=1000 and 71.5 % vs 60.1 % at C=5000, co300), and fewer new honest requests are admitted (59.9 % vs 82.7 % and 66.3 % vs 94.8 %). On the target topic, attackers hold 98.1 % (C=1000) and 97.0 % (C=5000) of the target's ads. Few target-topic honest requests get in: target_honest_admitted / target_honest_started (renewals included, from grid.csv) is 9.3 % at C=1000 and 46.7 % at C=5000. The target-topic honest p95 (54,653 s at C=1000, 1,320 s at C=5000) covers only those admitted; before #177 it was 1,314 s at C=1000, so the C=1000 p95 moves with a small admitted population rather than with the mechanism. The 37 % and 50 % in the "new honest requests admitted" row cover all honest topics.
- Diverse attackers (score near 0) keep about 80 % in every model when all requests give up, and reach 98.4 % (C=1000) and 95.5 % (C=5000) when only honest requests give up.
- With IP score off, attackers pay only base·G on their many sparse topics and stay under budget, so give-up barely limits them: 85.6 % (all give up) and 97.7 % (honest only) at C=1000, 92.5 % for both at C=5000.

Prefix sweep: /24, /16 and /8 give similar shares in every column, and diverse gives about 80 % in every column except go+honest-give-up.

## Change from #177 (topdisc 703d5a2 against the #166 run)

The first run of this grid kept a topic's bound after its last ad left (#166). #177 reverted that: the bound now goes with the topic's last ad. grid-pre177.csv and RESULTS-pre177.md keep the earlier run; onur-paper and onur-current rows are unchanged.

- IP score on (co300, co10k, tt, prefix sweep, Pocc sweep): the go columns move by at most 2.8 pp in attacker share (co300-p16 with honest give-up; at most 1.2 pp elsewhere) and 0.5 pp in utilisation. Honest p95 moves by at most 13 % (diverse attackers with honest give-up) and under 10 % elsewhere; target-topic p95 with give-up moves by 21 % at C=5000.
- IP score off, 10,000 attacker topics: attacker share at C=1000 falls from 97.7 % to 91.0 % and new honest admissions rise from 8.7 % to 43.5 %. At C=5000 the attacker share falls from 94.9 % to 92.4 %, utilisation rises from 66.6 % to 73.5 %, and new honest admissions fall from 46.2 % to 19.9 %. Under #166 the bounds of attacker topics also outlived their ads, which held occupancy lower; without them occupancy rises and the incumbency effect above takes over.
- tt with honest give-up at C=1000: target-topic honest p95 goes from 1,314 s to 51,628 s. It covers about 9 % of target-topic requests in both runs, so it is not a stable statistic.

## Review round 1

compare.py changed in two places and the go rows were rerun (grid-r0.csv and RESULTS-r0.md keep the previous run): an `existing` reply now counts as registered (see "Duplicates"), and a quote below 1 s is retried after 1 s, as the client clamps it (none occurred). Against the previous run, the go columns move by at most 1.1 pp in attacker share (tt with honest give-up at C=1000, 58.3 % to 57.2 %), 0.6 pp in attacker share within the target topic, and 0.5 pp in utilisation. Honest p95 moves by at most 10 % (diverse attackers with honest give-up at C=1000) and target-topic p95 by at most 9 % (tt with give-up at C=5000). The C=1000 tt honest-give-up target p95 (51,628 s to 54,653 s) is again the small-population statistic noted above.
